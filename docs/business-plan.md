# LaunchGrid Studio — Business Plan

Oct 12, 2026 · @Reytek

LaunchGrid Studio builds custom sites, starting with musicians: a one-time build fee is the business, and a small monthly care plan covers hosting, upkeep and the back office. Funkharp is the first build and the proof; labels and companies are the premium offer through LaunchGrid Hub.

## The business

One storefront, one company: launchgrid.studio, run as a trade name of Keymacro LLC, with music as the first section.

- **Brand:** LaunchGrid Studio at launchgrid.studio (domain already owned). The name fits any industry, so it won't be outgrown.
- **Company:** Keymacro LLC files a trade name ("doing business as") for LaunchGrid Studio, so no second LLC is needed. Confirm the Georgia filing and taxes with an accountant.
- **Money kept separate:** its own bank account (or at least its own invoices and books) so it's clear whether the studio pays for itself.
- **Site structure:** a studio home page, then a section per industry. **/music** launches first with Funkharp as the featured project; **/restaurants** and other sections are added only once there's a real build to show in each.
- **Positioning:** "Sites that feel like your band," not "we build websites." The Funkharp turntable playing on the page is the pitch.

## The offer

Artists get a site built around their own story and sound, done for them, plus a back office that replaces three or four separate tools. A do-it-yourself builder gives them a theme and a weekend of work.

|  | Custom band site (this) | Do-it-yourself builder (e.g. Bandzoogle) |

| Design | One of a kind, built from the artist's art, logo and story (Funkharp: turntable, harmonica player, pencil sketches) | Pick a theme, change colors |
| Who builds it | Me, start to finish | The artist |

The pitch in one line: *a site that feels like your band, that you never have to build or babysit.* Funkharp is the live example to show every prospect.

## LaunchGrid Hub: labels and companies

The premium offer: labels, management companies and venues get one hub for their whole roster, plus a site for each artist, quoted per project.

- **What the hub is:** the label's own site with every artist's music player, upcoming shows and videos pulled into one place, so fans can browse the roster and keep listening as they go.
- **One back office for the roster:** booking requests for every artist in one inbox, routed to the right person; one mailing list with each artist's fans tagged; shows and releases managed in one place.
- **Roster deals:** each artist gets a Headliner-level site at a volume price, on one bill to the label.
- **Pricing:** quoted per project. Starting points: hub from $5,000; artist sites in a roster deal from $1,500 each; one care plan for the whole roster from $150/month.
- **Why it's worth more:** labels have budgets, need more (several artists, team logins, routing), and value one vendor over five.
- **First step:** pitch it to 2–3 indie labels with Funkharp as the sample artist and a mock-up of a hub page. Their answers set the real prices.

## Costs and break-even

Care plans cover hosting from the third artist on; profit comes from build fees. Hosting is mostly a fixed cost of about $45 a month that every artist shares.

**Monthly hosting costs (from current pricing pages)**

- Vercel Pro: $20/month for my seat; covers every artist's site. Required because the free plan is personal, non-commercial only.
- Supabase Pro: $25/month, includes one small database. Each extra database is about $10/month, unless all artists share one.
- Resend (email): free up to 3,000 emails/month and 3 domains; Pro is $20/month once there are more than 3 artists sending email.
- Domains: each artist owns and pays for their own.

**Care plan income vs. hosting, assuming an average plan of $35/month**

**Where the money is:** eight artist builds a year at an average of $1,800 = $14,400, plus one LaunchGrid Hub at $5,000+, before care plans. At 10–15 hours a build, that's well over $100 an hour; track hours on artist #2 to confirm.

## How a build runs

Seven steps from first call to launch; the artist's homework is front-loaded so the build never stalls waiting on them.

1. **Discovery call (30 min, free).** Their story, sound, shows, what they use now. Show Funkharp live. Pick a tier.
2. **Proposal + deposit.** One-page proposal with tier, price, timeline. 50% deposit starts the work.
3. **Gather the material.** One checklist email: logo and art, photos of the current lineup, music files with song names, member names and instruments, show dates, links, domain registrar.
4. **Design concept.** A clickable prototype of the home and music pages, built around one signature idea from their art. Revisions per tier.
5. **Build.** Site from the template, music uploaded, shows and videos loaded, back office set up (Tier 3).
6. **Review on the demo link.** Artist plays every song and checks every page; one fix list.
7. **Launch.** Connect their domain and email sending, final 50% due, 15-minute back office walkthrough, care plan starts.

## Terms

Simple, written down once, and fair to both sides: artists own their stuff, I own my time and my template.

- **Payment:** 50% to start, 50% at launch. For artists on a tight budget, the build fee can split into 3 monthly payments, with launch after the last one.
- **Care plan covers:** hosting, security and software updates, backups, keeping the music player and forms working, and up to 1 hour a month of small changes (photo swaps, new show dates, a new song).
- **Not covered:** new pages, redesigns, new features. Quoted first, never surprise-billed.
- **Ownership:** the artist owns their domain, music, photos, art, words and mailing list. I own the template and code behind it and license it to them for as long as they're on a care plan.
- **Leaving:** they can leave any time with 30 days' notice. I export their mailing list, bookings and media, and point their domain wherever they want.
- **Late care plan payments:** 30 days' grace, then the site goes to a simple holding page; their domain and data stay theirs.
- **One-page agreement** for every build, signed with the deposit and issued by LaunchGrid Studio (a trade name of Keymacro LLC).

## Making it repeatable

Turn Funkharp into a template before artist #2, so each new build is mostly design and content, not plumbing.

- **Reusable as-is:** the site-wide music player and pocket player, booking letter, mailing list, back office (inbox, fans, shows, music, videos), phone alerts, auto-reply, link previews, admin logins.
- **Swapped per artist:** colors, fonts, logo, photos, copy, music, and one signature piece (Funkharp's turntable and harmonica tab bar).
- **One shared database for all artists** instead of one each: keeps hosting near $45–65/month at 10 artists and makes updates one job instead of ten. Decide this before artist #2.
- **One code base, one setting per artist** (name, colors, links), so a fix ships to every site at once.
- **Signature piece library:** each new signature piece (a turntable, a cassette deck, a jukebox) can be reused as a Stage look later, and in LaunchGrid Hub artist pages.

## Risks and open questions

The biggest risk is underpricing the build; the rest are manageable with written terms.

| Risk | What could happen | Fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build fee too low | Custom pieces like the turntable eat 2–3× the hours planned | Track hours on artist #2; reprice tiers after it |
| Small bands can't afford $1,800 | Lost deals at the first call | 3-payment plan; Stage as the entry point |
| Scope creep | "One more thing" requests during the build | Rounds of changes fixed per tier; extras quoted |
| Hosting prices change | Vercel or Supabase raise prices | Care plans reviewed once a year; shared database keeps exposure low |
| Artist stops paying the care plan | Hosting cost with no income | 30-day grace, then holding page |
| Only me doing support | Weekend emergencies before a show | Care plan promises next-business-day replies, not 24/7 |

**Open questions**

- Funkharp took about 10–15 hours. Is that typical, or was it fast because it was a first build with no template to set up? Time artist #2 by step.
- Do labels actually want a roster hub, and at what price? Ask 2–3 indie labels before building one.
- Which industry is second after music (restaurants, local businesses)? Only add it once there's a build to show.
