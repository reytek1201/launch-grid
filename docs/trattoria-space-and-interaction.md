# Part 1. The space (the brand bible for every image)

Lock this before generating any dish. Every image, from the front door to a plate of pasta, should look like it was shot in the same room on the same night.

## 1.1 The place

Trattoria Cinque Lune is a family kitchen in a 1920s brick corner building. Cozy, a bit worn, loved.

| Element | Lock |
| --- | --- |
| Walls | Hand-troweled limewash in warm ochre-terracotta, patches of exposed aged brick |
| Ceiling | Dark oak beams |
| Floor | Worn terracotta tiles |
| **Brand motif** | **Five round paper lanterns hung in a row** (the five moons). Appears in the dining room, hero, logo and Il Conto |
| The long table | One long communal oak table, mismatched wooden chairs |
| The oven | Arched brick wood-fired oven with visible flames |
| Props | Copper pots, strings of dried chilies and garlic, brass candle sconces, candles in wine bottles, jars of tomatoes, olive oil tins |
| Tableware | Cream ceramic plates with a thin terracotta rim, brass cutlery, linen napkins, simple wine glasses. The same set in every dish photo |
| Palette words | cream, terracotta, olive green, espresso brown, touches of wine red |
| Light | Warm candlelight (about 2700K) plus soft golden window light; low-key, gentle shadows |
| Camera feel | 35mm documentary photograph, shallow depth of field, Kodak Portra 400 color, fine film grain, natural, lived-in |
| People | **Hands only, never faces.** Avoids uncanny AI faces and any likeness issues |
| Text | **No text or signage in images.** AI garbles letters; names and signs are added in HTML/SVG |
| Time of day | Late afternoon into evening (it ties to the scroll: the page warms as the meal goes on, see Part 2) |

## 1.6 Files, credits and honesty

- **Keep the repo light.** Originals stay outside the project (your Fal downloads folder or cloud storage). The site uses optimized copies, roughly 200-300 KB each: about 30 images, around 8 MB total.
- **Naming:** `sp-03-dining-room.png`, `d-03-tagliatelle-ragu.png` (ID, then a short name); crops carry a ratio suffix (`-4x5`, `-21x9`, `-3x2`, `-16x9`, `-1x1`). Masters are PNG; optimized JPG/WebP copies are made when images are wired in.
- **Record every image** in `restaurants/clients/demo/assets/image-log.md` (ID, file, model, settings, references, cost, date, alt text) with prompts in `prompts.md`. All images are labelled "AI-generated".
- **Label the demo.** The demo site says "Demo restaurant, not a real business" and "Images are AI-generated" in the footer.
- **Real clients are different (direct advice).** Don't show AI-generated dishes as a real restaurant's food. For paying clients, use their real photos for food. AI is fine for textures, backgrounds and mock-ups, if the client agrees and it's disclosed. Ask a lawyer how advertising rules apply before you sell it any other way.
- **Placeholders:** until a photo arrives, each slot shows a tinted block in the right ratio, so the build never waits on images.

# Part 2. The interaction system: "Candlelight and ink"

The goal you described: hover, scroll and everything else should feel like one thing. The way to get that is not more effects. It is **one idea, one set of physics, applied everywhere.**

## 2.1 The idea

The restaurant is lit by candles and written in ink. Two things happen everywhere:
- **Light responds to you.** Your pointer is a candle; the page warms as the meal goes on.
- **Things are drawn and laid, not popped in.** Lines are inked in, plates are set down.

## 2.2 One shared clock

A single value, `meal` (0 at the door, 1 at the check), is computed once from scroll position and shared by everything. It drives:
- the page tint, easing from afternoon cream toward warm evening (text colors stay inside the checked contrast pairs);
- lantern glow, rising through the page;
- the course tracker on the left edge;
- the scene choreography.

That is what makes it feel connected: the lantern in the door glows brighter at the same moment the table fills up and the background warms.

## 2.3 Hover vocabulary (same physics everywhere)

Same easing as Funkharp's `Reveal` (`0.2, 0.7, 0.2, 1`), three durations only: **0.25 s hover, 0.6 s reveal, 0.9 s scene.** Terracotta always means "alive, you can touch this".

| Element | Hover / focus | Press / tap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text links | Hand-drawn underline draws left to right | Underline fills |
| Buttons (Reserve, Order, Call) | Magnetic pull toward the pointer plus an ink fill sweep | Slight press-in |
| Menu row | Dotted line draws from dish to price, row lifts 2px, a small photo peeks beside the cursor (desktop) | Row expands to show description and photo (touch) |
| Course heading | Handwritten flourish draws under it | n/a |
| Polaroid (gallery) | Straightens, lifts, shadow deepens | Opens lightbox |
| Dish tickets | Tilt toward the pointer, up to 6 degrees | Flip to the back for detail |
| Door sign | Swings | Toggles hours popover |
| Check tray | Opens like a folded check | Stays open until closed |
| Lanterns | Glow brightens when the pointer comes near | n/a |
| Keyboard focus | **Same state as hover**, plus a visible focus ring | Same as press |

## 2.4 The candle cursor (desktop with a mouse only)

A soft warm light follows the pointer with a little lag. It brightens over interactive things and dims over empty space. It is off on touch screens, off with reduced-motion, and the page reads fine without it (it is only extra light, never the thing that reveals content).

## 2.5 Scroll vocabulary

- Reveals are staggered per course, not all at once.
- Illustrations stroke-draw as they enter; handwritten words write themselves.
- Photos drift slightly against the page (about 24px at most); no heavy parallax.
- Scenes pin only on desktop, and the sideways table is the only horizontal moment.
- On phones, scroll position replaces hover: the dish nearest the center of the screen gets the "hover" treatment.

## 2.6 Guardrails

- Animate only transforms and opacity. One shared animation loop, not one per element.
- No layout shift. Mid-range phone is the test device, at 390px width.
- Reduced-motion: everything is already in its final state.
- Hours, phone, address, and the Reserve / Order / Call buttons always work without any of this.
- Lazy-load the heavy pieces (scenes, cursor) after the first paint.
